The one rule

Is this better for the elephants?

Every decision here answers that question first — every building, every path, every booking, every dollar. If the answer is no, we don't build it. That single rule is why there are no rides, no shows, no bathing, no feeding, no touching, and no promise that you will see an elephant at all.

How the rule works in practice

From the field log

Some mornings, you will see nothing at all.

Our care team keeps a daily log. We publish it unedited — including the days when the herd stays in the treeline and no guest sees an elephant. Those days are not failures. They are the whole idea working.

The land

Most of this place is not for you.

Of a hundred and fifty acres in central Florida, roughly a hundred and thirty belong entirely to the elephants: oak hammock, open grassland, a lake, mud wallows, shade trees, enrichment stations, a veterinary barn and a retirement barn.

Everything guests use — the glass pavilion, the cabins, the café, the shop, the spa rooms — sits together in a compact village on one edge, screened by trees and out of earshot. You never cross into their land. They can always walk out of sight of yours.

Where guests do observe, it is because we built the trail near a place elephants already like — a watering hole, a stand of shade, a wallow. We went to them. They were never brought to us.

Two species, one mission

Everyone here is recovering from something.

Our elephants arrive from circuses, roadside display, riding camps and closed zoos. Our guests arrive from burnout, grief, anxiety and the kind of year that doesn’t end cleanly.

We are not going to pretend those are the same wound. But the treatment turns out to look alike: space, quiet, time, good food, no demands, and nobody watching you to see whether you’re better yet.

They rest from

Circuses, riding camps, roadside attractions, neglect, decades of being useful

You rest from

Burnout, stress, anxiety, grief, a transition you didn’t choose

Shared prescription

Land, water, shade, silence, an unhurried day, and no obligation to perform

The pavilion

A glass room facing a wild horizon.

The pavilion is the heart of the guest experience — a glass-walled hall set on the treeline, open to the light, quiet enough to hear the lake. Yoga at sunrise. Pilates mid-morning. Restorative practice at dusk. Sound baths after dark. Talks by the people who care for the herd.

Sometimes elephants move across the far field while you practise. Sometimes they don't. Either way, the hour is the same.

How this is paid for

We sell everything except the elephants.

Sanctuaries that depend on visitors seeing animals eventually start managing animals so visitors see them. We removed that pressure by building the revenue somewhere else.

Cabins, memberships, retreats, weddings, the café, the shop, donations, grants and sponsorships all carry the cost of care. No income line anywhere in this business depends on an elephant being visible, cooperative, or anywhere near a human being.

Where the money goes

  • Cabin stays and private buyouts
  • Wellness memberships, local and remote
  • Hosted and private retreats
  • Weddings and small ceremonies
  • The café and the gift shop
  • Individual and monthly donations
  • Named sponsorships and grants
  • The care endowment, held for the long term
What we're actually building

An elephant can live sixty years. We are planning for the last thirty of them.

Restoration is not trying to be the biggest sanctuary. It is trying to be the one that is still standing, still funded, and still worthy of trust when the animals we take in today are old.

Come once as a guest. Stay for life as a protector.
