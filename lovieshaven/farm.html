Winnie’s Farm  ·  Animal rescue

The elephants keep their distance. The goats never got the memo.

Winnie’s Farm sits on the east side of the estate, along the service road — a rescue barn and paddocks for farm animals with nowhere else to go, and the one place at Lovie’s Haven where hands-on is the whole idea.

Who lives here

Rescues, every single one of them.

Goats from a closed petting zoo. Sheep nobody sheared. Two donkeys who arrived inseparable and have stayed that way. Pigs, chickens, and a handful of sweet old horses whose working days are long behind them.

The farm is named for Winnie, the first goat through the gate, who ran the place within a week and has opinions about visitors, feed buckets and everything in between.

Here's the lovely thing: where the herd asks for distance and gets it, the farm animals ask for company — and they get that instead. It's the same rule with the opposite answer, because the question is always what's best for the animal. And a rescue goat's honest answer is: scratches, please.

Mornings at the farm

Feeding rounds are open to anyone willing to carry a bucket.

Every morning the farm team does the rounds — hay, feed, water, eggs, and a health check disguised as a scratch behind the ears. Guests are welcome to join all of it. We provide the boots; you bring the enthusiasm.

It's completely different from the other side of the estate, and that's the point. Kids who spend a morning mucking out with the goats seem to understand, better than any sign could explain, why we leave the elephants in peace in the afternoon.

What the farm is for

A second rescue mission, and an honest pressure valve.

The farm exists first for its own residents — they needed a home, and we had one. But it quietly protects the herd, too.

Almost everyone arrives at an animal sanctuary wanting to touch an animal. That's a wonderful instinct — it just can't land on an elephant. At Winnie’s Farm, it lands on a goat who is absolutely delighted about it, while the herd across the estate grazes on, entirely undisturbed.

The farm runs on the same open books as everything else: feed, farrier and veterinary costs are published, and the eggs, honey and wool go to the café and the shop, with the profits going to the care endowment.
