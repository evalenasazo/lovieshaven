The Founding 100

Be one of the hundred people who make this real.

Lovie’s Haven is a big dream — 150 acres, a herd, a pavilion, a village — and we want to be completely honest about what it costs. The full build is more than any single raise can cover. The Founding 100 is the part that has to come first: the land, and the sanctuary itself. And we're inviting exactly one hundred people to be the ones who did it.

The ask

$4.375 million from one hundred founders — the money that makes it real.

Here's the honest picture: the full estate — pavilion, bungalows, village — is a fifteen-to-twenty-million-dollar build, and no single raise was ever going to cover all of it. The Founding 100 funds the part everything else depends on.

Founding capital buys the land and builds the sanctuary itself: elephant-grade fencing, water, wallows, the barns, the farm. We won't borrow against any of it — we never want a loan payment competing with an elephant's care budget. Once the land is ours and the herd's home is funded, we raise the rest the way institutions do: named gifts, grants, and partners who want their name on a building, phase by phase.

The Founding 100 opens once, fills once, and closes forever. After the hundredth member joins, there will simply never be another — no matter what comes later.

What all one hundred hold.

The tiers set your nights. Everything else is the same for every founder, because there's only one Founding 100 and every member of it matters equally.

And underneath it all sits one promise: your money builds the sanctuary first. Your benefits begin the day the gate opens — and they'll mean something because you're the reason it opened.

  • Lifetime access to all classes, unlimited, any day the pavilion stands
  • Your annual stay, every year, for life — two, three or four nights by tier
  • Your name in the Sky View Pavilion, on the founders' wall, permanently

Where the money goes

Phase One is the sanctuary. Founders fund all of it.

Every founding dollar goes to the herd's side of the plan — the land under everything, and the things an elephant actually needs. We'll refine the budget against the final property, share it with founders line by line, and keep publishing the real numbers as we build.

Phase Two — the Sky View Pavilion, the bungalows and huts, the village, the endowment — is the capital campaign that follows, funded by named gifts, grants and partners. That campaign becomes possible the day the founders put real land under it. That's the gift of going first.

The honest arithmetic

Why lifetime benefits don't bankrupt the sanctuary.

A promise of "free stays for life" is only worth making if the numbers truly work. Ours do, and we'd rather show you the whole calculation than ask you to take our word for it.

All one hundred founders together use 275 room-nights a year. Our eleven cabins offer roughly four thousand. That means founders fill about seven percent of the estate's nights — the rest are sold to guests, and every sold night funds elephant care. Classes cost us nothing extra: the instructor is already teaching, and the room already faces the meadow.

So founders aren't a cost this place carries. They're its foundation — a hundred people with a lifetime reason to love and protect it.

How it works

Five simple steps between this page and opening day.

1 · Reserve

Tell us which tier feels right. A reservation is just a conversation and a letter of intent — no money moves yet.

2 · Agree

Every founder signs the same written founding-membership agreement: your benefits and our obligations, spelled out in plain language.

3 · Escrow

Your funds are held safely in escrow until the raise completes and the land closes. If either doesn't happen, every dollar comes back to you.

4 · Build

You'll follow the build the way sponsors follow the field log: honestly and completely — budgets, actual costs, delays and all.

5 · Open

Your benefits begin opening week and never end. And so does the herd's retirement — the thing you really built.
