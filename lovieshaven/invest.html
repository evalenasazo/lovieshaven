The Founding 100

One hundred people put the land under it. Everything else stands on that.

Lovie’s Haven is a massive project — a hundred and fifty acres, a herd, a pavilion, a village — and honesty about the price is the whole pitch: the full build runs well past what any single raise covers. The Founding 100 is the raise that has to come first — the land, and the sanctuary itself.

The ask

$4.375 million from one hundred founders — the money that makes it real.

Let us be straight about scale: the full estate — pavilion, bungalows, village — is a fifteen-to-twenty-million-dollar build, and one raise was never going to cover it. The Founding 100 funds the part everything else depends on.

Founding capital buys the land and builds the sanctuary itself: elephant-grade fencing, water, wallows, the barns, the farm. No bank touches that side of the fence — debt service must never sit on an elephant's care budget. Once the land is real and the herd's side is funded, the estate around it is raised the way institutions raise: named gifts, grants and partners who want their name on a building, priced properly and built in phases.

The membership itself opens once, fills once, and closes forever. Nobody joins the Founding 100 after the hundredth member, whatever the later phases cost.

Where the money goes

Phase One is the sanctuary. Founders fund all of it.

Every founding dollar lands on the herd's side of the plan — the land under everything, and the infrastructure an elephant actually needs. The budget is refined against the final property, published to founders line by line, and re-published as actuals while we build.

Phase Two — the Sky View Pavilion, the bungalows and huts, the village, the endowment — is the capital campaign that follows: named gifts, grants and partners. That campaign becomes credible the day the founders put real land under it, which is exactly the point of going first.

The honest arithmetic

Why lifetime benefits don't bankrupt the sanctuary.

A promise of "free stays for life" is only worth making if the model can carry it. Ours can, and here is the whole calculation.

All one hundred founders together hold 275 room-nights a year. Eleven cabins offer roughly four thousand. Founders occupy about seven per cent of the estate's nights — the rest are sold, and every sold night funds care. Classes cost the pavilion nothing extra: the instructor is already teaching, and the room already faces the meadow.

The founders are not a liability on this model. They are its proof — a hundred people with a lifetime reason to protect the place.

How it works

Five steps between this page and the gate opening.

1 · Reserve

Tell us your tier. A reservation is a conversation and a letter of intent — no money moves.

2 · Agree

Every founder signs the same written founding-membership agreement: your benefits, our obligations, in plain language.

3 · Escrow

Funds are held in escrow until the raise completes and the land closes. If either never happens, every dollar goes back.

4 · Build

Founders get the build the way sponsors get the field log: unedited — budgets, actuals, delays and all.

5 · Open

Your benefits begin opening week and never end. So does the herd's retirement, which is the thing you actually bought.
