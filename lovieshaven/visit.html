Visiting & questions

“Will I see the elephants?”

It's the first thing everyone asks, and it deserves a straight answer: almost certainly — most days, for as long as you care to watch — and never once on a schedule. Here's how that works, along with honest answers to everything else we're asked.

The honest answer

Almost certainly. Just never on demand.

The Elephant View Meadow and the watering hole sit directly in front of the Sky View Pavilion because the herd loves that ground — we built the glass where the grazing already was. Most evenings, they're somewhere in the picture.

What we can't give you — what we'll never give anyone — is a guarantee. Nobody here will call an elephant in, lead her somewhere visible, or so much as angle a light her way. So the sighting you get is completely real. That's exactly what makes it worth having.

Don't take our word for it: read the field log. Our care team publishes it unedited, every day — the evenings the herd grazed right up to the pavilion lights, and the mornings they kept to the grove. Both are in there, because both are the truth.

  • The pavilion glass. Meadow and watering hole straight ahead, all day and into the evening.
  • Your own porch. The far shore of the lake belongs to the herd, so the view sometimes has elephants in it.
  • The boundary walk. Included with every stay, led by a keeper who knows where the herd has been all week.
  • The barn's viewing wall. Everyday care — foot work, health checks — watched quietly whenever it happens naturally.
  • The Sunset Dock. Evenings over the water, when the herd often drifts down to drink.

Every one of these is a seat placed where the elephants already love to be. None of them moves an elephant an inch.

The other days

A quiet meadow is the system working.

Once in a while, the herd spends a day or two deep in the forest grove, and the meadow sits empty. If that happens during your visit, we won't apologize — it means four elephants are doing precisely what they feel like doing, which is the entire reason this place exists. We publish those days in the field log too, and we're a little proud of them.

Everything else

Honest answers to the questions we're asked most.

Can I touch, feed or bathe an elephant?

No — and it's a loving no. For our residents, being touchable was the job: decades of rides, baths, tricks and photo lines. Retirement means that part of her life is over, for good. Her peace is the whole gift, and we won't take a piece of it back for anything.

If your hands need an animal to fuss over — and we understand completely — Winnie’s Farm is full of rescued goats, donkeys and old horses who genuinely want the attention. Contact lives there, happily.

Other places offer elephant encounters. Why won't you?

Because an encounter has to be produced. Somewhere behind it, an elephant is being kept willing to stand close to strangers, and that willingness always costs her something. The sanctuaries we most admire keep their herds entirely away from the public, and they're right to. We simply built the version where you can still be near enough to watch a herd live — through glass, across a meadow, on their terms.

Can I visit just for the day?

There are no day tickets, because there are no tickets. The only people on this land are overnight guests, members here for a class, and the team who works here — that's how the property stays small enough that the herd has never had to notice it.

The ways in are a stay, a membership, or one of the twelve wedding weekends a year. We know that makes us harder to see. We think it's why we're worth seeing.

Can I bring my children?

From age twelve, staying in the Long House or on a full buyout — the quiet here asks a lot of little ones. And in truth, the best hour of a child's visit usually happens at Winnie’s Farm, where the residents' whole philosophy is scratches, please.

When am I most likely to see them?

We can offer patterns, never promises. Early mornings often find the herd at the watering hole; evenings tend to draw them toward the meadow as the day cools; midday Florida heat usually means the shade of the grove. The keeper on your boundary walk will tell you where they've been all week — and then the elephants will do whatever they please, which is as it should be.

Can I take photographs?

From anywhere you're welcome to be, as many as you like — some of the meadow photos guests take from the dinner table are extraordinary. What we never do is stage one: no elephant is positioned, lit or held still for a camera, and there are no photo sessions with the herd at any price. Drones stay grounded, everywhere on the estate, always.

Can I volunteer with the elephants?

The herd is cared for by a small professional team who live on site, and keeping that circle tiny is part of how the elephants heal. The kindest ways to help are sponsoring a resident or giving to their care — though at the farm, between us, there's usually a feed bucket that could use carrying.

How do I know the elephants are truly well?

We'd rather show you than tell you. The field log is published unedited every day. An independent welfare audit is published annually. All care follows protected contact — the standard the world's most respected sanctuaries use — and we're building to Global Federation of Animal Sanctuaries standards, with accreditation to be sought as soon as we're eligible. The full picture is on our about page.

How do I book?

Write to us at stay@theloviesfoundation.org with your dates and we'll take it from there — a proper booking room is being built, but for now it's a person answering, which we secretly prefer anyway.

Still wondering something?

Ask us. Really.

Write to stay@theloviesfoundation.org and a real person will answer — including the questions that start with “this might be silly, but…” Those are usually our favorites.
