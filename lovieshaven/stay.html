Stay

Eleven cabins between the lake and the meadow.

Rooms are deliberately few. The cap is not scarcity marketing — it is the number of people this estate can hold while still feeling, at seven in the morning, like it belongs to the herd.

Where you'll be

A hundred and fifty acres of oak hammock, grassland and water, in central Florida.

The property backs onto a national forest, which means the horizon behind the herd is protected land and always will be. Live oaks, palmetto, a spring-fed lake, and the particular quality of light Florida gets at six in the morning.

The cabins

Built low, built quiet, built to disappear into the treeline.

Cedar, lime plaster, stone floors, deep windows. Linen and wool. No televisions, no minibars, no in-room music, no corridors. Every porch faces water or meadow.

Six huts

Village Huts

Studio and one-bedroom huts at the quiet edge of the village, minutes from the Sky View Pavilion and the café. Wood stove, outdoor shower, a porch built for sitting still. Sleeps two.

From $1,060 a night

Four bungalows

Water Bungalows

One- and two-bedroom bungalows over the water — more apartment than cabin, with a living room, a private dock, and a deck with a soaking tub. The far shore belongs to the herd, so the light out there changes all day and occasionally has elephants in it. Sleeps up to four.

From $1,960 a night

One house

The Long House

Four bedrooms, a kitchen, a long table and a fireplace. Taken whole by families, retreat groups and wedding parties. Sleeps eight.

From $2,400 a night

Two-night minimum. Rates include breakfast, all pavilion practice, and the meadow walk. A conservation levy of $40 per night goes directly to the care endowment.

A day here

Nothing is compulsory. Nothing is rushed.

Wake when you wake. Practice runs from before sunrise until after dark, and you can attend all of it or none of it.

Breakfast is served at the café from seven. Lunch is a long table at one. Dinner is at seven-thirty, four courses, mostly grown within an hour of here. Then a talk in the pavilion, and then the property goes quiet.

Included

Breakfast, all group practice, boundary walk with a keeper, evening talks

Extra

Treatments, private instruction, lunch and dinner, cellar list

Evenings

The Sunset Dock from six; fire pit and stargazing from nine, unhosted and entirely optional

Quiet hours

21:00 to 07:00, property-wide

Not permitted

Drones, amplified sound outdoors, vehicles past the village, crossing the habitat line

Children

Welcome from twelve, in the Long House or by full buyout

Dogs

Not on this property, for reasons the herd would explain if they could

The whole place

Private buyout

All eleven cabins, the Sky View Pavilion, the café and the full team, for up to twenty-two guests. Used for family gatherings, company retreats and the occasional wedding weekend.

The rules do not change for a buyout. The habitat stays closed, the curfew stays at nine, and the sound limits at the boundary stay exactly where they are.

From $16,500 a night, two-night minimum

  • Exclusive use of the wellness village
  • Dedicated chef and dining programme
  • Private practice schedule built around your group
  • Keeper-led interpretation and a private evening talk
  • Ten per cent of the buyout fee to the care endowment

Two nights is enough to change how you think about a place. Four is enough to change how you spend.
