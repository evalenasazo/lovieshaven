The Founding 150

It all starts with a hundred and fifty people.

The Founding 150 are the people who make Lovie’s Haven happen. One hundred and fifty founding memberships — a $7.5 million raise — buy the land and build the sanctuary itself: the habitat, the water, the barns, the farm. The one rule

Is this better for the elephants?

Before we build anything, book anything, or spend a dollar, we ask that question first. If the answer is no, we don't do it. It's why you'll never see rides, shows, bathing or petting here — and why, when the herd wanders down to graze in front of the pavilion most evenings, it's because they love that meadow, not because anyone led them there.

How the rule works in practice

The land

One estate, laid out around the herd.

A hundred and fifty acres in central Florida: a conservation zone and a forest grove for the elephants, a lake with its own watering hole, the open Elephant View Meadow, and the retreat threaded carefully along the water's edge.

Guests live on the lake side — the Sky View Pavilion at the centre, the lakeside cabins and the guest village around it, the Sunset Dock for the end of the day. The herd's ground runs north and east: the grove, the conservation zone, the watering hole, and the open meadow they cross between them.

That's the quiet secret of the whole design. Elephants love meadows and water, so we put the pavilion where it faces the meadow and the water. When the herd wanders into view — and most days they do — it's because this is where they love to be.

Two species, one mission

Everyone here is recovering from something.

Our elephants arrive from circuses, roadside display, riding camps and closed zoos. Our guests arrive from burnout, grief, anxiety and the kind of year that doesn’t end cleanly.

An elephant's recovery and a person's are not the same thing, and we would never claim they were. But it turns out they call for a lot of the same medicine: space, quiet, time, good food, no demands, and nobody checking whether you're better yet.

They rest from

Circuses, riding camps, roadside attractions, neglect, decades of being useful

You rest from

Shared prescription

Land, water, shade, silence, an unhurried day, and no obligation to perform

The Sky View Pavilion

A glass room at the edge of the meadow.

The Sky View Pavilion is the heart of the estate — wellness, events and dining under one timber-and-glass roof, set where the gardens end and the Elephant View Meadow begins. Yoga at sunrise. Long-table lunches. Sound baths after dark. Talks by the people who care for the herd.

Most evenings the herd is grazing somewhere in the view. Some evenings they come all the way down to the watering hole. Either way, it tends to be everyone's favorite hour of the day.

Winnie’s Farm

The part of the sanctuary you can hug.

The elephants are not the only residents. Winnie’s Farm, on the east side of the estate, takes in rescued goats, sheep, donkeys and elderly horses — animals with nowhere else to go and, unlike the herd, a real enthusiasm for company.

It's the one place at Lovie’s Haven where hands-on is the whole idea. The elephants have more than earned their peace and quiet. The goats never got that memo — they'll be waiting for you at the gate.

How this is paid for

The retreat pays the bills. The elephants just live here.

When a sanctuary's income depends on visitors seeing animals, the animals slowly end up working for a living again. We never want that pressure anywhere near our herd, so we built the income around everything else instead.

Cabins, memberships, retreats, weddings, the café, the shop, the farm, donations, grants and sponsorships all carry the cost of care together. Not one dollar of it depends on an elephant performing, posing, or taking a single step she didn't choose to take.

Where the money goes

  • The Founding 150 — the capital raise that builds it
  • Cabin stays and private buyouts
  • Wellness memberships, local and remote
  • Hosted and private retreats
  • Weddings and small ceremonies
  • The café and the gift shop
  • Winnie’s Farm programs and produce
  • Individual and monthly donations
  • Named sponsorships and grants
  • The care endowment, held for the long term
What we're actually building

An elephant can live sixty years. We are planning for the last thirty of them.

We're not trying to build the biggest sanctuary in the world. We're trying to build the one that's still here, still funded, and still keeping its promises when the elephants we rescue today grow old.
