Café & shop

A flat white and a jar of honey are also conservation funding.

Two small businesses at the centre of the village. Neither of them is a gift shop bolted onto a cause — they are two of the eight lines that keep the herd fed.

The café

Open from six, for the people who wake before the light does.

Coffee and loose-leaf tea, smoothies and cold-pressed juice, a short breakfast menu, and a lunch that changes with what came in that morning.

Most of it is grown within an hour of here. The eggs, greens, citrus and honey are local by default; the rest we buy on quality, not on distance, and we tell you which is which on the board.

Guests eat here on their plan. Members eat at member rates. Nobody else is on the property, so there is never a queue.

The shop

Small, slow and deliberately hard to fill a bag from.

A single room beside the café. Linen, ceramics, blankets, books, the honey, and prints made from habitat footage. Made in small runs, mostly by people within a day's drive.

There is no plush elephant. There is no branded mug with a trunk on it. The residents' likenesses are not merchandise, and putting them on a keyring would undo a great deal of what the rest of this website is about.

Every dollar of margin goes to the care endowment. Not a percentage — the margin.

  • $18 — a jar of honey. Half a day of browse for one elephant.
  • $60 — a wool blanket. A quarter of a voluntary foot care session.
  • $140 — a habitat print. A week of hay.
  • $320 — the field log, bound annually. A full veterinary visit.

Prices are what the things cost to make plus what the elephants need. We publish the split.

Two small businesses, one very large obligation.

The café and the shop together cover roughly the annual cost of one elephant. That is not a rounding error in this model — it is a resident.
