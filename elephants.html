Our elephants

They have made their last journey. Nothing here will ask anything of them again.

Every elephant at Lovie’s Haven arrived from a life of work — circuses, rides, logging, roadside display. Here they have land, water, mud, shade, company if they want it, and the right to be left entirely alone.

How you'll come to know them

Through their stories, not their presence.

You will learn more about these elephants in a week here than you would in a year of standing at a fence — because we tell you about them properly instead of parading them.

Evening talks in the pavilion, where a keeper takes one resident and tells you everything: where she came from, what she was made to do, what she was like when she arrived, what she is like now. Footage from the habitat cameras, filmed without anyone present. A daily conservation session in the afternoon. And, when it happens naturally, the chance to watch ordinary care — morning feeding, a health check, an enrichment drop — from the barn viewing wall.

The observation trail was routed the same way a wildlife photographer would route it: we found the places the herd already chose — the watering hole, the deep shade, the wallow on the north slope — and we built the viewpoint back from them. Nothing was moved toward a guest. The guest was moved toward where life was already happening.

  • You will never touch an elephant here
  • You will never feed, bathe or walk with one
  • You will never be promised a sighting
  • No elephant will ever be moved so that you can see her
  • If she leaves, we let her go

These are not policies we intend to review.

The residents

Four elephants. Roughly a hundred and forty years of work between them.

The habitat is built for ten. We will fill it slowly, and only as fast as the endowment can carry each new arrival for thirty years.

Lovie

Asian  ·  47  ·  Arrived 2023  ·  From a travelling circus

Thirty-one years on the road. Lovie came to us underweight, with chronic foot damage and a habit of swaying that she has slowly stopped needing. She was the first elephant to walk onto this land — the haven is named for her — and she spent her first four days standing at the treeline looking at it without going in.

She now takes the long route around the lake every morning, apparently for no reason at all. That is the whole point of her.

Bala

Asian  ·  52  ·  Arrived 2024  ·  From a tourist riding camp

Bala carried people for twenty-six years. Her spine shows it, and always will. She is the most cautious of the four and the slowest to trust a new keeper, which we consider entirely reasonable of her.

She attached herself to Lovie within a fortnight, and in two years the two of them have almost never been more than a hundred metres apart.

Meera

Asian  ·  39  ·  Arrived 2025  ·  From a closed roadside attraction

Meera spent nineteen years in an enclosure smaller than the pavilion, alone. She arrived not knowing what mud was for. It took eleven months before she used a wallow, and the keeper who saw it happen had to sit down.

She is still deciding whether she likes other elephants. Nobody is rushing her.

Ruth

African  ·  58  ·  Arrived 2025  ·  From a zoo closure

The eldest of the herd and the reason the retirement barn exists. Ruth has arthritis in both front legs and comes in voluntarily most mornings for warmth, foot care and pain management. She has never once been made to.

Guests who watch her sessions from the viewing wall tend to be quiet for a while afterwards.

The field log

Published unedited, including the empty days.

Our care team logs what actually happens on the habitat. We do not curate it, and we do not leave out the mornings when the herd is nowhere to be seen.

14 July06:40

No elephants visible from the pavilion this morning. Lovie and Bala stayed inside the north treeline through breakfast. Guests drank coffee and watched the mist come off the lake instead. This is normal. This is the point.

12 July17:20

Meera went into the far wallow for the first time in eleven days and stayed forty minutes. Two of us watched from the service road. Nobody called anybody over.

11 July13:05

Browse delivery — willow and bamboo, dropped at four points across the range so nobody has to share if they don't want to. Bala got there first, as usual, and then didn't eat any of it.

09 July05:55

Foot care for Ruth, ninety minutes, voluntary. She chose to come in and she chose to leave. Six guests watched the session from the barn viewing wall and asked good questions afterwards.

07 July19:10

Rain all day. The herd spent it under the old oaks at the boundary. We cancelled nothing, because there was nothing scheduled to cancel.

04 July21:30

Wedding in the meadow, a hundred and twenty guests, ended at nine as agreed. Sound meter at the habitat line read below ambient all evening. Lovie and Meera were at the south lake and did not move once.

Sponsorship pays for her care, not for your access to her.

Sponsors receive her file — history, medical notes, keeper reports and habitat footage — four times a year. They do not receive a visit, a meeting or a photograph, because she is not available for those things and neither is anyone else here.
