Stay

Eleven cabins. A hundred acres you will never set foot on.

Rooms are deliberately few. The cap is not scarcity marketing — it is the number of people this land can hold without the herd ever registering that anyone arrived.

Where you'll be

A hundred and fifty acres of oak hammock, grassland and water, in central Florida.

The property backs onto a national forest, which means the horizon behind the herd is protected land and always will be. Live oaks, palmetto, a spring-fed lake, and the particular quality of light Florida gets at six in the morning.

The cabins

Built low, built quiet, built to disappear into the treeline.

Cedar, lime plaster, stone floors, deep windows. Linen and wool. No televisions, no minibars, no in-room music, no corridors. Every cabin faces away from the habitat.

Six cabins

Treeline

One room, one bed, one wall of glass facing woodland. Wood stove, outdoor shower, a porch built for sitting still. Sleeps two.

From $580 a night

Four cabins

Lake

Larger, set along the water with a private deck and a soaking tub. The far shore is habitat, so the light on that water changes all day. Sleeps two.

From $840 a night

One house

The Long House

Four bedrooms, a kitchen, a long table and a fireplace. Taken whole by families, retreat groups and wedding parties. Sleeps eight.

From $2,400 a night

Two-night minimum. Rates include breakfast, all pavilion practice, and the boundary walk. A conservation levy of $40 per night goes directly to the care endowment.

A day here

Nothing is compulsory. Nothing is rushed.

Wake when you wake. Practice runs from before sunrise until after dark, and you can attend all of it or none of it.

Breakfast is served at the café from seven. Lunch is a long table at one. Dinner is at seven-thirty, four courses, mostly grown within an hour of here. Then a talk in the pavilion, and then the property goes quiet.

Breakfast, all group practice, boundary walk with a keeper, evening talks

Dogs

The whole place

Private buyout

All eleven cabins, the pavilion, the café and the full team, for up to twenty-two guests. Used for family gatherings, company retreats and the occasional wedding weekend.

The rules do not change for a buyout. The habitat stays closed, the curfew stays at nine, and the sound limits at the boundary stay exactly where they are.

From $16,500 a night, two-night minimum

  • Exclusive use of the wellness village
  • Dedicated chef and dining programme
  • Private practice schedule built around your group
  • Keeper-led interpretation and a private evening talk
  • Ten per cent of the buyout fee to the care endowment

Two nights is enough to change how you think about a place. Four is enough to change how you spend.
