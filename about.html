About

Restoration is a sanctuary for elephants and a retreat for people.

We exist to restore dignity to rescued elephants and to create space for human peace. Those two things share a boundary fence and almost nothing else — which is exactly how it should be.

Philosophy

Humans adapt to the elephants. Never the other way around.

Our residents are not scheduled, cued, directed, moved for viewing, or asked to be anywhere at any time. Their day belongs to them.

What guests get instead is honest: the chance to witness natural life unfolding at a distance, on the herd's terms. Some mornings that means watching two elephants cross a field for twenty minutes. Some mornings it means an empty horizon and a keeper telling you what is probably happening in the treeline and why.

The understanding comes from storytelling, from the media we make ourselves, and from guided interpretation during ordinary care — never from arranging an animal for an audience.

  • No performances. Nothing an elephant does here is for a person.
  • No contact. No touching, feeding, bathing, walking or photographs with elephants.
  • No schedule. Sightings are never promised, timetabled or advertised.
  • No pursuit. If a resident moves away from view, that is the end of the encounter.
  • No crowds. Guest numbers are capped at a level the habitat has never noticed.
  • No breeding. We are a retirement sanctuary, not a breeding programme.
The design principle

Is this better for the elephants?

Every proposal at Restoration is tested against one question, and only one. If the answer is no, it does not get built — however much money it would make, and however much a guest might enjoy it.

The rule, applied

Things we chose not to build.

The elevated walkway

It would have carried guests over the wallows. It also would have put human silhouettes above the herd for the first time in their lives. Cancelled at plan stage.

Sunrise viewing slots

Bookable, premium, and instantly a schedule. The moment a sighting has a price and a time, someone has to make it happen. Never offered.

The lakeside restaurant

Beautiful site, wrong side of the water. Sound carries further over a lake than anyone expects. Moved inland, behind the trees.

The land plan

A hundred acres for them. A small village for us.

A hundred and fifty acres of central Florida — oak hammock, open grassland and a spring-fed lake, bordering a national forest. The property is divided once, permanently, and heavily in the elephants' favour.

Their side holds open range, the lake, mud wallows, shade trees, browse and enrichment stations, a veterinary barn and a retirement barn for residents who need warmth and closer monitoring in their last years. Access is limited to the care team and veterinary staff.

Our side is compact by design: the glass pavilion, eleven cabins, the café, the gift shop, treatment rooms, a meadow for ceremonies, and the parking that keeps every vehicle at the property edge. The forest boundary means there is nothing on the far side to develop, light or amplify.

Care standards

Protected contact, voluntary participation, no exceptions.

All handling is protected contact: keepers and elephants are never in the same unrestricted space. Every husbandry procedure — foot care, bloods, injections, weight checks — is trained through positive reinforcement and can be ended by the elephant at any moment simply by walking away.

Residents are never separated for human convenience, never moved for viewing, and never sedated for anything other than genuine veterinary need.

  • Full-time keeper team living on site
  • Retained large-animal veterinarian, weekly rounds
  • Independent welfare audit, published annually
  • Care endowment funded before any surplus is distributed
  • Intake decisions made on welfare grounds alone, never on story or fundraising value
On breeding

We considered it, and then we decided against it for good.

Breeding would change what this place is. It shifts the mission from rescue to production, adds a decade of operational complexity, and takes attention and money away from the elephants already waiting for somewhere to go.

If a rescued elephant arrives already pregnant, that becomes part of her story and we will care for both of them for life. That is a rescue outcome, not a programme. We may partner with conservation organisations in the future; we will not become a breeding facility.

What we are not

Not a zoo. Not an attraction. Not a day out.

There are no tickets, no gates, no queues and no visiting public. The only people on this land are guests who have booked to stay, members who have booked a practice, and the people who work here. That is not exclusivity for its own sake — it is the population cap the habitat was designed around.
